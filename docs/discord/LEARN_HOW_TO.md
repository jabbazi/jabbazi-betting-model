## 01 · PLACE YOUR FIRST BET, STEP BY STEP

1. Use a sportsbook you are eligible to use in your location. Complete its own account and identity checks. Never share your login with someone offering to bet for you.
2. Set aside a betting bankroll you can afford to lose. Keep bills and emergency money separate. Set your own deposit/spending limit.
3. Open the sport, then the correct game and date. Check whether the market is pregame or live, full game or a period.
4. Tap the exact selection and line. Check the sportsbook's rules for overtime, participation, postponements and voids.
5. In the slip, choose a single/straight unless you intentionally want a parlay. Enter the dollar stake calculated from your units.
6. Check the selection, odds, stake, potential PROFIT and total RETURN. If the price has moved past the post's play-to limit, pass.
7. Submit once. Open My Bets and confirm the accepted wager and price. Save the receipt. An unsubmitted slip is not a placed bet.

Practice: a 0.50u example with a $10 personal unit means entering $5, not $15.

## 02 · UNIT BETTING — YOUR STAKE SYSTEM

A unit (u) is a consistent dollar reference, not a confidence percentage. Stake = recommended units × YOUR unit size.

JABBAZI's current example: 1u = $30.
0.20u = $6 · 0.25u = $7.50 · 0.50u = $15 · 0.75u = $22.50 · 1u = $30.

Members choose a smaller personal unit when needed. For illustration, a $500 betting bankroll with a $5 unit makes 0.50u = $2.50. A $3,000 bankroll with a $30 unit makes 0.50u = $15. Both units are 1% of their example bankroll; that is an illustration, not a requirement or a guarantee of safety.

Our hierarchy: 0.25u speculative/small parlay, 0.50u qualified standard play, 0.75u stronger evidence, 1u rare highest-quality play. No tier is a lock.

Add up ALL your open stakes, including your own sprinkles. Five 0.50u bets risk 2.50u, and similar bets can lose together. Decide your spending limit before the slate. Never double stakes to recover losses or raise units mid-session out of frustration. Reassess your unit on a planned schedule.

## 03 · ODDS, PROFIT AND RETURN

American odds show payout, not certainty.
• +150: a $10 winning cash bet earns $15 profit; total return is $25 including the stake.
• -120: a $12 winning cash bet earns $10 profit; total return is $22.
• A losing cash bet normally loses its stake.

Decimal odds include the returned stake: $10 at 2.50 returns $25, with $15 profit. +150 = 2.50; -120 ≈ 1.8333.

For the SAME selection and line, +130 pays more than +110; -105 costs less than -120. Compare the exact market before comparing prices.

Break-even chance for a no-push cash bet: positive odds +A → 100/(A+100); negative odds -A → A/(A+100). -110 needs about 52.38% to break even, before any costs. The sportsbook's implied chance includes its margin and is not a proven model forecast.

A push is a tie under that market's rules; a void cancels a bet under the applicable rules. Straight-bet stakes are generally returned, while parlays can be repriced differently. Check your book's rules.

## 04 · READ A JABBAZI PICK CORRECTLY

Example only: Team A moneyline +120 | play to +110 | 0.50u | pregame.

Meaning: bet Team A to win that specific game, with a half-unit stake, only at +110 or better. +115 is acceptable; +100 is below the stated limit. A limit of -115 accepts -110 and rejects -125.

Before tailing, match the sport, date, team/player, market, period, line and odds. A different threshold is a different bet: Over 45.5 is not Over 47.5, even if the price looks similar.

BET NOW means the posted conditions must still hold. WATCH, WAIT FOR LINEUP/INJURY/WEATHER, UNAVAILABLE and PASS are not instructions to bet. A model percentage is an estimate, not a promise.

Cheat sheets show research snapshots. Check the timestamp and current price. Experimental/shadow models are not approved betting recommendations. Official posts, member plays and research remain separate. When an official play is updated or invalidated, read the update before entering a new wager.

## 05 · MONEYLINES

A moneyline is a bet on who wins the specified game or period. A favorite has a shorter payout; an underdog has a larger payout. Neither is automatically good value.

How: open the correct event → choose Moneyline → tap the team → check odds and period → enter your unit-based stake → review the slip.

Example: Team A ML +125, $10 stake. If it wins under the market's rules, profit is $12.50. The victory margin does not matter.

Check whether the market includes overtime/extra innings. A two-way market and a three-way market with a Draw selection settle differently. Soccer regulation moneyline usually has three outcomes; Draw No Bet is a different market. Read the market label rather than assuming.

## 06 · SPREADS, RUN LINES AND PUCK LINES

A spread adds or subtracts points/runs/goals for settlement.
• Team A -3.5: it must win by 4 or more.
• Team B +3.5: it wins if Team B wins outright or loses by 1–3. Losing by 4 loses the bet.
• At -3, a 3-point win can push in a standard two-way spread market.

How: choose the Spread market → check the sign and exact number → check the price beside it → enter the stake. The line and price are separate: -3.5 at -110.

MLB run line and hockey puck line use the same idea, often with 1.5 runs/goals. Team A -1.5 needs a win by at least 2. A plus spread does not mean an automatic win or a moneyline bet. Check period and extra-time rules.

## 07 · TOTALS AND TEAM TOTALS

A game total uses both teams' combined score. A team total uses only the named team's score.
• Game Over 47.5: 48+ combined points wins.
• Game Under 47.5: 47 or fewer wins.
• Team A Over 24.5: Team A needs 25+, regardless of the opponent's score.

How: open Totals or Team Totals → choose Over/Under → verify the number and full-game/period label → compare the odds → enter your stake.

A whole-number total can push when the result equals the line. Half points cannot tie. Check overtime/extra-inning rules. An over is not better just because you expect a high-scoring game: the posted threshold and price still matter.

## 08 · ALTERNATE LINES AND THRESHOLDS

Alternates offer different spreads, totals or player thresholds at different prices.

Example: a receiver's 50+ yards is easier to reach than 80+ yards, but the 50+ price may be much more expensive. An easier condition can still be poor value.

How: open Alternate Lines or the player's milestones → select the EXACT threshold in the official post → check odds → check whether it is a single or a parlay leg → enter the stake.

50+ means at least 50. Over 50.5 requires 51 or more for an integer stat. Over 50 can push at exactly 50 under the market's rules.

Do not substitute an alternate because it looks safer, or keep adding easy-looking legs. The full price, joint chance and total risk matter. Threshold ladders are multiple bets: add every stake together.

## 09 · PLAYER PROPS

A prop is a bet on a specific player's statistic: passing/rushing/receiving yards, receptions, attempts, strikeouts, outs, hits, points, rebounds or assists, depending on the sport and available markets.

How: choose event → Player Props → correct player → exact statistic → Over/Under or milestone → confirm threshold, odds and participation rules → enter stake.

Example: Over 4.5 receptions needs 5 catches. Receiving yards are not rushing yards; passing touchdowns are not anytime touchdowns. Combined stats such as PRA use the named sum only.

Playing time matters: starting role, snaps, routes, minutes, injuries and lineup changes can change the outlook. Check confirmed availability. Never assume an injured or early-exiting player automatically voids; book and market rules differ.

College-football coverage in this community focuses on game markets, not college player props. Teaching a market does not mean JABBAZI currently has a validated model for it.

## 10 · ANYTIME TOUCHDOWNS

An anytime-TD market asks whether the named player scores a qualifying touchdown. In usual scorer markets, a QB throwing a touchdown is not credited as the scorer; the receiver is. A QB rushing for one is different. Verify the book's exact scorer rules.

How: choose NFL event → Touchdown Scorers → Anytime → correct player → confirm price and settlement rules → stake.

Anytime, first touchdown, 2+ touchdowns and passing touchdowns are different bets. Do not select the wrong tab. Check whether overtime and specific return types count.

Touches near the goal line, red-zone routes and role matter; high yardage alone does not guarantee a TD. These bets have substantial variance. A TD parlay can lose even when every player has a good game. Keep longshot exposure small and never add legs just to create a bigger payout.

## 11 · MLB: F5, NRFI/YRFI AND PROPS

F5 means the first five innings. F5 moneyline, run line and total are separate markets; confirm how ties, shortened games and listed pitchers are handled. Full-game bets include later innings under their rules; F5 bets do not simply mean the full-game winner.

NRFI: no run scored by either team in the first inning. YRFI: at least one run in that inning. Select the right game and exact first-inning market. Neither is a safe bet just because it settles quickly.

Pitcher strikeouts count strikeouts; outs recorded count completed outs. 17 outs = 5 innings plus 2 outs. Baseball notation 5.2 innings is not 5.2 decimal innings. Workload and early removal matter.

Batter hits, total bases, RBIs, runs and home runs measure different things. Under standard total-bases definitions a walk is not a hit or a total base; confirm settlement rules. A home-run ladder creates multiple stakes.

How: select event → exact market → correct starting pitcher/batter → verify lineup, threshold, price and rules → review stake.

## 12 · FIRST HALF, QUARTERS AND OTHER PERIODS

A period bet covers only the named segment: first half, first quarter, first five innings, first set or another stated period. Moneyline, spread and total can each exist for that segment.

How: switch to the period tab → read the full market name → verify the event and selection → check tie/void rules → review stake.

Example: First-half Over 21.5 needs 22+ combined points in that half. A high-scoring second half cannot rescue it. First-quarter Team A -0.5 needs Team A ahead at the end of that quarter.

Period markets often have less data and more variance. Do not apply a full-game probability directly to a half or quarter. If the research for the requested period is unavailable, wait or pass.

## 13 · PARLAYS AND SAME-GAME PARLAYS

A standard parlay combines legs into one ticket; all active legs must win for the full payout. A same-game parlay (SGP) combines selections from the same event. Check special settlement rules for pushes and voids.

How: select each intended leg → open the parlay/SGP section of the slip → verify every event, period, threshold and combined price → enter the TOTAL ticket stake once. A 0.25u parlay means 0.25u on the ticket, not on each leg.

For independent legs only: two 60% chances combine to 36%, not 60%. Same-game legs often depend on one another, so simply multiplying their probabilities is unreliable. QB yards + receiver yards, for example, share game conditions.

Our focus is selective 2–3 legs, with 4 only when justified. Bigger payouts do not create an edge. Multiple parlays using the same player/team can all lose from one event. Count their stakes together with your straights. Member parlay posts are not automatically official JABBAZI picks.

## 14 · ROUND ROBINS AND TEASERS

A round robin places multiple smaller parlays from a set of selections. Three selections in 2-leg combinations create AB, AC and BC: three separate bets. If you enter $5 per combination, the total cost is $15. Always read whether the slip asks for per-bet stake or total stake. It is possible to win some combinations and still lose money overall.

A teaser combines adjusted spreads/totals under specific rules and a set price. It is not the same as picking any alternate lines. Check allowable sports, number of legs, adjustment, odds, and how ties/voids settle.

How: choose the specific bet type → inspect every generated combination or adjusted line → check TOTAL amount risked → submit only if the post explicitly calls for it.

These are optional advanced formats, not a requirement of our daily card. Do not convert a straight pick into a round robin or teaser and assume the original research still applies.

## 15 · PROFIT BOOSTS, ODDS BOOSTS AND BONUS BETS

Read eligibility, minimum odds, leg count, maximum stake, expiry and payout limits before building a ticket. Apply the offer in the slip and verify that the final price actually changed.

A profit boost increases qualifying winnings, not normally the returned cash stake. Example: $10 at +100 earns $10 profit; a 50% profit boost makes that $15 profit and $25 total cash return, if those terms apply.

An odds boost replaces the price. Compare it with other books; the word boost does not guarantee value.

A bonus bet may return winnings without returning the promotional stake. DraftKings' published bonus-bet rules use that treatment. A $10 bonus bet at +200 would then produce $20 winnings, not a $30 cash return. Other offers can have different terms.

How: open the reward → read terms → construct only qualifying selections → apply reward → check cash versus bonus amount, final odds and total exposure. Never add an unwanted leg just to use an offer.

## 16 · LIVE BETTING, CASH OUT AND HEDGING

Live odds move during the game, and a broadcast or score feed can be delayed. A pregame research price is not a current live price. Confirm the live score, clock, market period and accepted odds. Do not keep submitting when a price is rejected or moves past your limit.

Cash out is an offer to settle an open bet early. Availability and price can change or disappear. The offered amount is not necessarily fair value.

A hedge is a separate opposing bet that changes the payout in different outcomes. It adds another stake and may add sportsbook margin; it is not automatically a way to make the original wager profitable. Review the combined worst-case result before adding it.

How: calculate what each outcome pays across ALL related tickets → compare the offer or hedge cost → decide before placing anything. If the conditions or calculation are unclear, do not improvise. No chasing losses with late-game overs, bigger stakes or random hedges.

## 17 · VALUE, LINE SHOPPING AND YOUR RECORD

A likely winner can be a bad bet at the wrong price. For a no-push cash bet, expected ROI = model probability × decimal odds − 1. Example: a 60% estimate at 1.50 has -10% expected ROI; at 1.80 it has +8%. These are math examples, not real forecasts.

Probability edge and ROI are different. A 60% model estimate versus a 55% no-vig market estimate is a 5-percentage-point probability edge. It does not mean 5% ROI. Model errors can erase either apparent advantage.

Shop the SAME event, selection, line and rules across books. Record accepted price, stake, time and receipt. Later record win/loss/push/void, actual profit and closing price. Do not erase losses or rewrite old entries.

ROI = net profit ÷ total amount staked. Hit rate alone is incomplete: price matters. Closing-line value is useful feedback, not proof of profit or model accuracy. Include all your own wagers when judging your total bankroll risk. Passing is a valid result.

## 18 · NBA, TENNIS AND OTHER SPORTS

NBA: moneylines, spreads and totals work like other game markets. Player points, rebounds, assists, threes and combined props are different statistics. Confirm starters, minutes expectations and overtime rules.

Tennis: match winner, set winner, game handicap and total games are different markets. A match winner need not win the most total games. Retirement, walkover and tiebreak rules can materially affect settlement; read your book's tennis rules.

Soccer: distinguish regulation 1X2 (home/draw/away), Draw No Bet and To Qualify. A team can advance after extra time while failing to win its regulation-moneyline bet.

Hockey: distinguish regulation markets from moneylines including overtime/shootout. UFC: moneyline, method, round and fight-duration markets settle on different conditions. Futures lock up a stake until a later outcome.

How for any new sport: identify what must happen to win, exactly when the bet ends, how unusual results settle, and the total amount at risk. If you cannot explain those four things, ask before betting. A listed lesson is not a claim that the scanner currently models that sport.

## 19 · QUICK CHECK BEFORE EVERY BET

Can you answer YES to each?
• Correct event, date, player/team and period?
• Exact market, threshold and price?
• Within the official play-to limit, if following an official post?
• Stake converted using YOUR unit size?
• All open/related tickets counted toward your own risk limit?
• Participation, overtime, postponement and void rules understood?
• Accepted ticket saved after submitting once?

If not, pause. Never bet bill money, borrow to chase, or treat subscription fees as a reason you must place bets. Take a break when betting is affecting your sleep, mood or finances. Use your sportsbook's limit and exclusion tools when needed.
